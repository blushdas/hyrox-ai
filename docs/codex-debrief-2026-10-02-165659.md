## What Was Implemented
Received text begins revealing without an initial hold. Each reveal tick uses actual elapsed time divided by 16 ms, clamped to one through four frames. Its step is the larger of a character per frame or one twelfth of the pending text scaled by elapsed frames, rounded up and capped at 400 characters. The hook skips callbacks under 16 ms, so a 120 Hz display does not double the reference cadence. The arrival estimator is gone.

Each newly revealed batch gets a stable identity and a 140 ms fade using the existing ease-out curve. Only opacity and transform are animated. The same Markdown renderer handles live and settled blocks, preserving column width, spacing, bold, lists, links and citations. The renderer maps formatted leaves to the received-text ranges so syntax characters do not shift the fade onto older text. Batches older than 140 ms merge into settled text; the long-paragraph browser probe kept at most nine live spans and recorded no restarted animations. Finalized blocks remain static and memoized. Reduced motion shows all received text with no reveal loop or fade.

Stop dispatches a render-layer flush before calling the existing stop handler. It reveals all received text immediately and clears the fade batches; the store and stream code are unchanged. A trailing `[` or numeric fragment such as `[1` is withheld until the closing bracket arrives. The caret still appears when the live tail is empty.

Normal completion keeps revealing through the remaining backlog and lets the last fade finish before displaying the Take card. A 1,350 ms finish guard flushes exceptionally large remaining backlogs, matching the reference's finish-then-flush design. A fixed 400-character frame cap cannot also reveal an arbitrary million-character backlog within 1.5 seconds; the guard is an explicit exception to the per-frame cap and no-dump rule, not an arrival-rate adjustment. The browser hook's million-character guard probe used metadata only and did not paint a million-character paragraph.

The assistant preserves the largest streamed content footprint for its current mount. The final compact Take card sits at the bottom of that reserved space. This keeps document height and scroll position from shrinking during the swap; height is not animated. Foreground browser measurements saw a 865 px card inside a 1780 px reserved footprint, with no backwards scroll samples. Mount position, short upward pauses, Latest visibility, error cancellation and earlier-message memoization from round 1 are retained.

### Existing pacing tests explicitly superseded
The prior tests require an arrival-rate estimator and its `rate`, `arrivalRate` and `carry` state; those fields no longer exist. Their one/two-character adjacent-step variance bounds conflict with the requested backlog-proportional formula, which intentionally takes a larger step after a burst. Their `revealFrames(0) === 0` and `revealFrames(8) === 0.5` assertions conflict with the reference's minimum of one frame. The prior pure-function promise to drain up to one million characters within 1.5 seconds conflicts with the new fixed 400-character cap; it is now checked as a cap/deadline conflict in the pure tests and handled by the hook's finish guard. The old bursty 1,500-character fixture's assertion that over 500 characters must remain pending encoded the slower estimator and is replaced by an explicit large-backlog completion test. These are deliberate changes required by Troy's new instruction, not silently relaxed assertions.

There are 21 new/updated pure tests: exact reference stepping, the cap, no overshoot or backwards progress, sustained burst arrivals, a 1,500-character terminal backlog at 30/60/120 Hz, frame clamping, aged batch bounds, text-range intersections and incomplete citations. Existing non-pacing tests remain passing.
